Direct Bluetooth from the Fitbit Air

Can OpenAir skip the Google Health cloud? · research follow-up · 9 Oct 2026 · ← back to the main plan

VerdictThe two doorsPhone platformsWhat we can compute1-hour testPlan impact

Partly. The Air can broadcast live heart rate over standard Bluetooth (the "Share heart rate" setting), probably including beat-to-beat RR intervals. That's enough to compute real-time HRV and stress on your phone with no cloud involved.

The band's history can't be read locally yet. That includes sleep stages, nightly HRV, SpO₂, skin temperature and steps. It travels inside an encrypted sync format that open-source researchers have connected to but haven't decrypted (status as of 29 Sep 2026).

Recommendation: hybrid. Live data goes straight from the band; history comes from the Google Health API. If the encryption is ever broken, a local adapter can replace the cloud.

The two Bluetooth "doors" on the band

Door A · Standard GATT open

0x180D Heart Rate Service
0x2A37 measurement: bpm · contact · RR intervals*
0x180F Battery · 0x180A Device info

Advertised only while Share heart rate is on ("Always visible" keeps it on). The band may need to bond (pair) first. Drains battery. Usually one connection at a time.

Door B · Fitbit "GoldenGate" locked

Gattlink transport
DTLS, per-device "MD-…" key (extractable only from the app on a rooted phone)
CoAP
Sync dumps with inner encryption: key on band + Google backend only

Air researchers reached an authenticated connection from Linux, then stalled at the inner encryption. Charge 6 work gets live snapshots only, not sleep, HRV or SpO₂.

Heart Rate Measurement wire format

flags (1 byte): bit0 HR uint16 · bit1-2 sensor contact · bit3 energy present · bit4 RR present
heart rate:     uint8 or uint16 bpm
[energy]:       uint16 kJ (if bit3)
RR intervals:   uint16 each, units of 1/1024 s  → ms = raw × 1000 / 1024
Conflicting report: one reviewer says the Air "does not support" the standard HR profile on Garmin and Wahoo head units, while apps and an open-source project read it fine. The likely cause is that the Air requires an encrypted, bonded link, which phones handle and some bike computers don't. The test below settles it for your band.

Why not break into Door B anyway?

  • It needs a rooted Android phone for key extraction, and onboarding still goes through Google.
  • Even the furthest-along project (Charge 6) can't decrypt sleep, HRV or SpO₂. Google's server answers those sync blobs.
  • Any firmware update can break it, and it may violate Google's Terms of Service. US law has an interoperability exemption (DMCA §1201(f)) that may cover personal use; this isn't legal advice.
  • A second bonded app may interfere with Google Health's own syncing.

Plan: watch Gadgetbridge #504 and keep a slot in the architecture for a local sync adapter.

What we can compute from live HR + RR

Live HRV

RMSSD and SDNN over a rolling 1–5 min window, with artifact rejection. Only trusted while you're still.

Real-time stress

HRV below your baseline and HR above resting for 5–10+ min. The phone's motion sensors help separate stress from exercise, since the HR broadcast has no accelerometer data.

Our own overnight HRV

Record RR all night (phone or bedside hub), then take the calmest stable windows. Costs battery.

Instant alerts

No waiting on cloud sync. For example: "HR +35 over resting for 10 min while seated, HRV −40%. Take a breather?"

1-hour test on your Air (no code)

  1. In Google Health → your Air → Connections, turn on Share heart rate and Always visible.
  2. Install nRF Connect (Android or iOS). Scan for the Air advertising 0x180D, connect, and accept pairing if asked.
  3. Subscribe to 0x2A37 and read the raw bytes. Is bit 4 of the first byte set? If so, RR intervals are present.
  4. Sit still and breathe slowly. Do the RR values vary beat to beat? If they always equal 60000 ÷ bpm, they're synthetic and useless for HRV.
  5. While connected, check that Google Health still syncs. Leave Always visible on for 24 h and note battery use.
